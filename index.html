Mathematics is the foundation of data science, yet most texts either sacrifice rigor for accessibility or accessibility for rigor. This book does neither.

Starting from datasets and the question of what it means to average, the text develops linear geometry, matrix decompositions, calculus, probability, and statistics in a unified geometric framework, culminating in a mathematically rigorous treatment of machine learning. Every concept is motivated by data, every theorem is proved from first principles, and every proof is accompanied by working Python code.

The approach is geometric throughout. Eigenvectors are maximum variance directions. The pseudoinverse is the "closest twice" solution. The singular value decomposition is rotation, scaling, rotation. Information and the cumulant-generating function are convex duals. Trainability — the question of whether gradient descent converges — is characterized precisely in terms of the geometry of convex hulls.

Several results appear here for the first time: the trainability of logistic loss and its dichotomy with perceptron loss, matrix reduction as a unifying framework for EVD and SVD, and a four-way equivalence characterizing full-rank datasets in terms of linear algebra, span, hyperplanes, and convex geometry.

Designed for graduate programmes in Applied Mathematics, Computer Science, Data Science, Engineering, and Business Analytics, the text supports both a leisurely one-semester course through the decomposition chapters and an advanced one-semester course through the machine learning chapters — or both semesters taught simultaneously.
